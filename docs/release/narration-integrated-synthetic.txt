This is VORQYN for the MLH Solana Hackathon.

A storm-damaged port needs air, ground and underwater inspection. When a sensor becomes unreliable, operators need to understand what was observed and what failed.

I start our synthetic survey and advance it. This is our original fictional port. The ROV follows kernel-observed coordinates; the clock is simulator time, not blockchain time. These are not physical robots or SITL.

I inject an underwater optical fault. The next measurement shows a sharp optical quality drop. Sonar remains independent of that fault, with its own synthetic noise. These measurements are not evidence verdicts.

Replay re-executes the actual operation trace and returns identical observations. The two-dimensional alternative preserves source-labelled robot state without WebGL.

Now I configure and save a distinct mission brief, then reload it. Regions, sonar requirements, thresholds and an exact proposed budget persist in this browser. This is not company authentication, funding or launch.

Our merged escrow foundation has local VM tests; our source-aware journal has replay and ordering guards. Devnet deployment, reassignment, payout, independent verification and runtime AI are not demonstrated here.

Codex and ZCode assisted development. Event work and external dependencies are disclosed in the repository. This is a working synthetic inspection prototype with explicit release limitations.
