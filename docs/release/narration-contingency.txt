This is VORQYN for the MLH Solana Hackathon.

After a storm, a port needs inspection above the cranes, along the access roads, and beneath the water. Different machines see different parts of the problem.

VORQYN brings that work into one understandable environment. This is our original fictional port, with aerial, land and underwater camera stations. These machines are static reference anchors, not live telemetry.

The same metadata drives an accessible two-dimensional fallback, so losing WebGL does not lose the inspection context.

Here is a separate, real run of our deterministic synthetic kernel. I inject a severe underwater optical fault. The measured optical quality falls sharply; sonar remains an independent sensor. This terminal demonstration is not integrated browser playback, physical hardware, an AI decision or a verifier verdict.

Now I create a local mission brief: choose an objective and actual port region, specify a sonar requirement, set acceptance thresholds and propose an exact budget. Save, reload, and the distinct editable draft is still here. It is saved in this browser, not a company account, and is not funded or launched.

Devnet escrow, reassignment and payout are not connected in this release. We are showing the working planning, world and synthetic foundations honestly, with reproducible checks and clear integration boundaries.
